Plan a home solar installation

Moving to solar power starts with a few practical checks. Review recent utility bills, note how much electricity your household uses in different seasons, and decide whether your goal is lower grid use, backup power, or both. A local installer can use this information to prepare a system estimate, but the estimate depends on the home and the utility rules in your area.

Check your roof and utility bill

Look for shade from trees or nearby buildings and ask when the roof was last repaired. The roof may need work before panels are installed. A qualified contractor can inspect its structure, but this page cannot determine whether a particular roof is suitable. Keep a recent bill nearby so a proposal can be compared with actual use.

Compare installer proposals

Ask each installer to describe the equipment, expected system size, warranty terms, permit responsibilities, and assumptions behind projected savings. Compare the same details across proposals. Savings estimates are not guarantees: electricity prices, household use, weather, financing, and local policy can change the result.

Before signing, check the permit process with your local authority and ask the utility how a new system connects to the grid. Make sure the written proposal explains who handles inspections, what happens if the roof needs repairs, and how service requests are handled after installation.
